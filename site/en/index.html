About us

A living network for Brazil's coral reefs

The Brazilian Coral Reef Coalition is a multisector collaboration dedicated to the conservation, restoration and appreciation of the country's coral reefs. We emerged from the meeting of science, communities, the private sector, academia and civil society, with the purpose of joining forces around marine life and the sustainability of Brazil's coastal ecosystems.

The Coalition began taking shape in 2022, based on a mapping of existing initiatives and consultation with representatives of the private sector, the nonprofit sector, academia, government and local communities.

More than a network of partners, we are a collaborative movement working in an integrated and transparent way to strengthen the national and global coral conservation agenda.

Our commitment is to act as an integrating platform, connecting existing initiatives, avoiding duplication of effort and strengthening conservation actions. We seek to expand impact, influence public policy, support solutions and strengthen Brazil's leadership on the international ocean agenda.

Brazil's Reefs

Caring for corals means caring for ourselves.

Coral reefs are more than ecosystems: they are living networks that teach us about interdependence and resilience. They are important for marine biodiversity, coastal protection, the food security of traditional communities and the tourism economy.

Biodiversity

They occupy less than 0.2% of the ocean floor and support more than 30% of known marine species.

Coastal protection

They reduce wave energy and help protect beaches and cities.

Food security

They support fishing and provide food for traditional communities.

Economy and culture

They drive tourism and are part of the identity of many territories.

Did you know?

Corals live in partnership with microscopic algae called zooxanthellae. Through photosynthesis, the algae provide nutrients and oxygen, while the corals provide shelter in return. This partnership gives the reef its color and energy.

Mission, vision and purpose

What drives us

Mission

Promote the conservation and restoration of coral reefs in Brazil by bringing together key stakeholders and integrating initiatives to strengthen the national and global agenda.

Vision

Brazil's coral reefs are recognized as essential ecosystems for biodiversity and the coastal economy, receiving sustained investment and support to ensure their protection and long-term resilience.

Purpose

Bring together diverse sectors (civil society, academia, the private sector and local communities) under transparent and robust governance to catalyze practical action, influence public policy, drive innovative solutions and ensure the financial sustainability of coral-reef conservation, restoration and appreciation, aiming to position Brazil as a leader in the global coral conservation agenda.

Our values

Diversity

We are a diverse and equitable movement made up of multiple stakeholders.

Cooperation

Our foundation is cooperation among partner organizations to develop sustainable and innovative solutions for Brazil's coral reefs as a shared goal.

Dialogue

Our actions are grounded in empathy, dialogue and assertiveness to resolve potential conflicts and keep work flowing smoothly.

Action and Leadership

We act to develop consistent solutions, closing gaps and strengthening coral reef conservation on the national agenda.

Our actions · Working Groups

From global commitment to local action

We follow the global Coral Reef Breakthrough targets, adapted to the reality of Brazilian reefs. Each thematic pillar has a Working Group where Impact Network organizations build collaborative initiatives and achieve concrete results.

The Impact Network is organized into five Working Groups, one for each thematic pillar. Within the WGs, member organizations develop initiatives, share experiences and coordinate joint action.

Each WG brings together at least three organizations that have joined the Coalition: one coordinating organization and at least two member organizations, with a lead and alternate coordinator.

  1. Pillar 1 · WG 1

    Threat Mitigation

    We halt local drivers of reef loss, such as human-caused pollution, unplanned coastal development, invasive species and overfishing.

    Working Group: For organizations working to reduce pollution, unplanned coastal development, invasive species and overfishing in reef areas.

  2. Pillar 2 · WG 2

    Expanding Protection

    We expand and strengthen existing Protected Area systems in reef regions, recognizing and respecting the rights of Indigenous Peoples and Traditional Communities, in line with the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework.

    Working Group: For organizations working to expand and strengthen Protected Areas in reef regions.

  3. Pillar 3 · WG 3

    Accelerating Restoration

    We support science-based coral restoration strategies, environmental safeguards and recognition of traditional knowledge.

    Working Group: For those researching, implementing or supporting coral restoration initiatives.

  4. Pillar 4 · WG 4

    Mobilizing Investment

    We help mobilize public and private resources for coral conservation and restoration, connecting funders with those implementing work on the ground.

    Working Group: For funders and implementing organizations connecting public and private resources with coral conservation.

  5. Cross-cutting pillar · WG 5

    Monitoring

    We establish and monitor indicators of conservation effectiveness, creating a trusted space for data sharing and strengthening existing projects and networks as well as those that may be established in the future.

    Working Group: For those who produce, organize or share data and indicators on the effectiveness of reef conservation.

Network governance and participation

Network-based governance

The Strategic-Deliberative Committee sets priorities. The Executive Secretariat coordinates and integrates the Working Groups. The Impact Network turns strategy into action in each territory.

How your organization participates

  1. 1

    Express interest

    Send a message and indicate the Working Groups you are interested in.

  2. 2

    Formalize membership

    Sign the Membership Agreement and commit to the Coalition's principles and values.

  3. 3

    Join a WG

    Participate in at least one Working Group. Each WG has a lead and alternate coordinator and meets at least every two months.

Applicant organizations may undergo due diligence and integrity checks.
