Why are we talking about corals at the Ocean Decade?

The UN Decade of Ocean Science for Sustainable Development (2021–2030), the ‘Ocean Decade’, coordinated by UNESCO's Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission, exists to turn ocean science into the solutions people and nature need. In April 2027, Rio de Janeiro hosts its global Conference, where priorities for the rest of the Decade, and its legacy beyond 2030, will be shaped. Coral reefs are where that promise is tested first. They are among the ecosystems hit hardest and fastest by ocean warming, and among the most valuable to people. If science, communities and policy can work together anywhere, they have to work here.

And why Brazil?

Brazil's reefs are the major reef systems of the South Atlantic and are home to coral species found nowhere else on Earth. Hosting this conversation in Rio puts the South Atlantic, and the Tropical Americas and Caribbean, at the centre of the global reef agenda.

Our Special Event

Coral Reefs at the Frontline

Individual Sessions

← That's you

An Individual Session is one component of a full-day Special Event. Sessions are proposed through an open call run by the Conference organizers, then woven into one coherent programme by the Lead Partner, here Coalizão Corais do Brasil and partners. Session outcomes feed directly into the Conference discussions and the Ocean Decade's legacy.

How the day works

  1. Coalition block

    1. Opening plenary & provocative pitches

    Short perspectives from scientists, Indigenous and community leaders, youth/Early Career Ocean Professionals and policymakers.

  2. Open call

    2. Individual Sessions

    Selected through the open call. This is where your session fits.

  3. Coalition block

    3. Networking & partner showcase

    Time and space to meet, display initiatives and build collaborations.

  4. Coalition block

    4. Coral Reef 2030 Action Lab

    An interactive synthesis of priorities, partnerships and commitments emerging from the day.

  5. Public

    5. Coral reef photography exhibition

    A public-facing show on bleaching and conservation, open to the wider Rio de Janeiro community.

The Coalition leads a block of about 3 hours (opening, networking and Action Lab); the rest of the day is built from Individual Sessions. The final running order is set after selection.

What the day will produce

A Coral Reef 2030 Action Agenda

Input to the Ocean Life Solution Blueprint and the Tropical Americas & Caribbean Regional Blueprint

New partnerships and commitments through 2030

Joint proposals are strongly encouraged. We especially value sessions co-organized across institutions, countries and sectors.

Particular consideration is given to meaningful participation from Small Island Developing States (SIDS), Least Developed Countries (LDCs), Indigenous Peoples, Early Career Ocean Professionals (ECOPs), youth, under-represented regions and Brazilian institutions.

  • Climate impacts & adaptation
  • Reef monitoring & knowledge systems (including traditional and local knowledge)

Propose a session

The essentials

Apply on the official form, not to us.

Proposals are submitted only through the Conference's online form. We can't accept proposals by email.

Choose our Special Event.

In the form, select Special Event No. [XX]: “Coral Reefs at the Frontline: Connecting Science, Communities and Policy for a Resilient Ocean”.

Write in English.

All form responses must be in English.

Deadline: 8 November 2026, 23:59 UTC (20:59 Brasília).

Late proposals cannot be considered.

Who decides.

Sessions are selected by the Conference organizers; Coalizão Corais do Brasil reviews and recommends proposals for our day. Results are expected in December 2026.

Answer three guiding questions.

Every session should address: What are the priority gaps? Which solutions can scale? What partnerships are needed? Your answers feed the Coral Reef 2030 Action Agenda.

Plan your own budget.

The Ocean Decade does not fund infrastructure, logistics or catering for these events. Plan your own travel and participation costs. [COST-SHARING: to be confirmed]

No automatic Conference pass.

Running a session does not guarantee accreditation to the main Conference (7–9 April).

Open to registered participants.

Special Events are open to registered Conference participants on a first-come, first-served basis unless otherwise agreed.

Share your outcomes.

Selected organizers share their session's key outcomes with us for the event report and the Conference's legacy documentation.
